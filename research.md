# 🔬 Research Overview

My research focuses on the **chemical transformation of heterogeneous waste streams** — particularly municipal solid waste (MSW) and lignocellulosic biomass — into value-added chemicals, functional materials, and recoverable polymers. I work at the interface of materials chemistry, reaction engineering, and green solvent systems, with an emphasis on mechanistic understanding, selective chemical conversion, and structure–property relationships.

The central goal is to develop scalable, chemically driven pathways that convert complex, contaminated feedstocks into platform chemicals and materials with defined molecular and morphological characteristics.

---

## 🧪 Core Research Themes

### 1. Solvent-Targeted Recovery of Post-Consumer Plastics

I investigate selective dissolution and precipitation strategies for recovering polyolefins (primarily polyethylene and polypropylene) from mixed municipal solid waste. This work examines:

- Solvent–polymer interactions and solubility parameters of aromatic and non-aromatic solvents
- Influence of temperature, solid–liquid ratio, and impurity profiles on extraction yield and selectivity
- Molecular-weight distribution and thermal stability of recovered polymers
- Downstream processability of solvent-recovered resins into films and composites

The chemistry centers on controlled phase behavior, polymer–solvent thermodynamics, and the removal of additives and contaminants that affect material performance.

---

### 2. Thermochemical Conversion of Biomass and Biogenic Waste

I study the pyrolysis and catalytic upgrading of lignocellulosic and mixed biogenic fractions of MSW to produce biochar, condensable liquids, and non-condensable gases. Key chemical questions include:

- Pathways of cellulose, hemicellulose, and lignin depolymerization under inert and catalytic conditions
- Evolution of aromaticity, porosity, and surface functionality in biochars
- Role of in-situ and ex-situ catalysts in directing product selectivity toward platform chemicals
- Coupling of thermal analysis (TGA, DSC) with evolved-gas analysis (py-GC-MS) to map reaction networks

This line of work links solid-state carbon chemistry with gas-phase and liquid-phase product distributions.

---

### 3. Deep Eutectic Solvents for Biomass Fractionation and Platform Chemical Synthesis

I use deep eutectic solvents (DES) as tunable, low-toxicity media for the selective conversion of xylan and other hemicellulose-rich feedstocks into furfural and related oxygenates. Research activities include:

- Design of hydrogen-bond donor/acceptor pairs that modulate acidity and polarity
- Kinetic studies of xylan hydrolysis and subsequent dehydration to furfural
- Spectroscopic and thermal characterization of DES–biomass interactions
- Solvent recovery and recyclability under realistic process conditions

The chemistry focuses on controlling reaction selectivity through solvent microstructure and acid catalysis while minimizing degradation pathways.

---

### 4. Structure–Property Relationships in Waste-Derived and Bio-Based Polymers

I formulate and process recovered and bio-derived polymers into films, molded articles, and composite systems. Emphasis is placed on:

- Correlating molecular architecture (branching, molecular weight, crystallinity) with thermal and mechanical behavior
- Use of rheology, DSC, DMA, and FTIR to map processing windows and end-use performance
- Incorporation of biochar and other carbonaceous fillers to tune conductivity, reinforcement, and barrier properties
- Evaluation of biodegradation and long-term stability under accelerated aging conditions

This work translates molecular-level chemical insight into macroscopic material performance.

## 🎯 Research Philosophy

I approach waste valorization as a **chemical engineering problem rooted in molecular reactivity**. Rather than treating waste as a bulk feedstock, I seek to understand and exploit the specific chemical functionalities present in plastics, polysaccharides, and lignin-derived structures. The long-term objective is to design conversion systems that are selective, energy-efficient, and compatible with existing industrial infrastructure — thereby accelerating the transition from linear disposal to circular chemical manufacturing.

- Reaction engineering and kinetic modeling of biomass and plastic conversion systems
- Design of task-specific solvents and catalysts for selective depolymerization
- Advanced characterization of carbonaceous materials and polymer composites
- Scale-up and technoeconomic evaluation of solvent- and thermochemical-based recovery processes
