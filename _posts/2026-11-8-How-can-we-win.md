title: How Can We Win?
---

*"The borrower is servant to the lender." --- Proverbs 22:7*

Our elders had a saying: **"A man may own a house, but the house can own
the man."**

Housing is a basic human necessity. Yet try to buy a modest \$300,000
home at 7.5% interest. With a 20% down payment, the mortgage alone costs
roughly \$604,000 over thirty years. Add property taxes, insurance,
utilities and repairs, and the lifetime bill can approach a million
dollars.

And when the mortgage is finally paid? The bills remain. The government
still collects its taxes. Insurers still collect their premiums. The
house still needs heat, water, repairs and care. Even passing it to your
children can involve legal and administrative hurdles.

As another old saying goes, **"The more you owe, the more you serve."**

One could almost think the Republic designed the arrangement this way:
let citizens spend their strongest working years chasing a home, then
keep collecting long after the bank has been paid. Perhaps nobody needs
to conspire. The system can produce the same result all by itself.

So, how can we win?

Perhaps winning begins by refusing to confuse ownership with freedom.
Buy only what leaves room to live. Understand the full cost, not just
the monthly mortgage. Avoid becoming house-rich and cash-poor. Build
savings beyond the walls. Know the tax and inheritance rules. And when
possible, work together---as families, communities and neighbours---to
demand housing that serves people before it serves balance sheets.

The old wisdom says, **"United we stand, divided we fall."** Maybe that
is where our leverage begins: not in chasing the biggest house, but in
questioning why a basic human need has become a lifelong financial
contest.

A home should shelter a life---not consume it.

**How can we win? By making sure that, in the pursuit of a place to
live, we do not surrender the very life we hoped that place would
protect.**
