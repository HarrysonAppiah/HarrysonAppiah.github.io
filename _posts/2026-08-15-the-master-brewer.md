There is a distillery in our brains.

Lade Worsonu’s *The Master Brewer* begins with this simple but powerful image. Our lives, he suggests, are made from the things we experience—the victories, disappointments, uncertainty, luck, loss, and everything in between.

> *“There is a distillery in our brains  
> Its cane and malt, its hops and grains  
> Are the stuff our lives are made of.”*

I find this especially relevant today. We spend so much time trying to control what happens next: our careers, our finances, our families, our plans, and our futures. Yet life has a way of reminding us that control is often an illusion. A storm can change a plan. A single decision can open—or close—a door. Sometimes success comes down to preparation; sometimes, as Worsonu reminds us, it can be as unpredictable as a coin toss.

But perhaps that is not the most important question.

The question is: **What do we do with what life gives us?**

We may not get to choose every ingredient, but we have some influence over what we brew from them. Our failures can become lessons. Our disappointments can become perspective. Our successes can become humility. Even uncertainty can become an invitation to grow.

The future will always contain things we cannot predict. Perhaps wisdom is not learning how to control the future, but learning how to meet it.

**What are you brewing with the ingredients life has given you?**

*— Monday Musings*
